Fake News Detection is the process of using automated, computational systems to identify and flag intentionally false or misleading information presented as legitimate news. In the era of rapid social media sharing, these systems are vital for maintaining an informed public and combating the spread of misinformation.

The core challenge is that fake news is often crafted to look real and trigger strong emotions. Detection systems rely on sophisticated Machine Learning (ML) models, which are trained on vast amounts of verified real and fake articles to learn patterns.

Detection focuses on three main areas of analysis:
1. Content Analysis

This looks at what the text actually says. ML models, often using Natural Language Processing (NLP), check the article's structure, style, and meaning. They flag content that uses overly sensational or emotional language, poor grammar, or incoherent claims. A key technique is Stance Detection, which verifies if the headline's claim is genuinely supported by the article's body.
2. Source Analysis

This examines who published the information. The system checks the credibility and history of the publishing website or author. Sites with a known history of bias or misrepresentation are given a lower trust score.
3. Propagation Analysis

This focuses on how the news spread across social networks. Suspicious activity includes rapid, non-organic sharing, or a high concentration of sharing by bot accounts, which suggests manipulation rather than genuine public interest.

By combining the findings from content, source, and propagation analysis, the system calculates a probability score to determine if a story is fabricated, helping platforms stop the spread of harmful disinformation.
